REPLYINPUBLIC

A public comment inbox for brands. Formerly Overheard.

6,900+ Comments 17 Kitchen Brands 5 Brands in Inbox Built Solo HackUMass XIV
VISIT REPLYINPUBLIC.US VIEW THE CODE DEVPOST

The site is public. The inbox needs a sign in and shows complaints, questions and buyers for five of the brands.

BRIEFING

Brands pay for surveys to find out what customers think, and I've seen them hand out discount codes just to get one filled in. Customers already post that feedback in public.

ReplyInPublic puts those public comments in one inbox for the brand, so money spent asking can go to answering.

ROLE / METHOD / OUTCOME

Role
Solo build at HackUMass XIV, November 2026.
Method
Timed collectors, a noise rule, Gemini labels and issue grouping, on PostgreSQL.
Outcome
A live inbox at replyinpublic.us, on real public comments.

HOW IT WORKS

1. COLLECT

Timed workers pull public comments about all 17 brands from YouTube. Instagram, TikTok, X and Reddit cover five of those brands, unevenly.

2. CHECK FOR A REPLY

It records whether the brand already replied. On YouTube a reply only counts when it comes from the brand's own channel. On the other platforms it's mostly unknown.

3. LABEL

A code rule marks obvious noise. Gemini labels the rest as a complaint, question, purchase intent, praise or noise.

4. GROUP

For KitchenAid, Nespresso, Breville, Instant Pot and Ninja, Gemini groups complaints, questions and buyer comments into named issues where they fit. The inbox has two sign ins, one for a brand team and one for the ReplyInPublic team.

HOW IT STARTED: OVERHEARD

From July to November 2026 this project was called Overheard. It was a study of the customer questions and complaints brands miss on their own social posts. For HackUMass XIV I built it as an inbox in a new repo and renamed it ReplyInPublic.

THE DATA

It collected 3,097 YouTube comments on 21 brands through the official API, and 791 Instagram comments on 17 kitchen brands through Apify.

THE METHOD

The comments landed in BigQuery. Gemini on Vertex AI tagged type, sentiment and urgency. The Instagram comments aimed at a brand were clustered into themes with KMeans on embeddings and given a 0 to 100 response priority score.

WRONG ACCOUNTS

5 of the 17 brand handles were wrong or impostors before they were checked. De'Longhi could only be measured once its dead handle was fixed. In July, all 37 comments aimed at it were complaints.

REPLIES THE SCRAPER MISSED

34 comments flagged as ignored were checked by hand in the live threads. Some had brand replies the scraper couldn't see, so "no reply found" was never proof of no reply.
