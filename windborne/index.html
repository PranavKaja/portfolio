How to play Semantle, from someone who just learned

Semantle hides one word. You guess words, and it tells you how close each guess is in meaning, not in spelling. It learned meaning from a huge amount of news text, so two words count as close when they show up in the same kinds of sentences.

Every guess gets two numbers. Similarity runs up to 100, and the page tells you what the nearest word scores that day, so you know what warm looks like. In my game the nearest word scored 79.6 and the thousandth nearest scored 27.42, so anything under about 20 was cold. Once a guess lands in the top 1,000 words, you also get a rank out of 1,000, and a higher rank means closer.

Here is the strategy I would give someone new.

  1. Open wide. Start with broad words from different parts of speech, like time, person, money, make, think, good and usually. I did the opposite. My first ten guesses were things around me, like ball, pen, fruit, phone and building, and all ten were cold. Phone even came back below zero. A specific object only tests one small corner of the language, so a cold one tells you very little.
  2. Use a hint when you are stuck. A hint gives you a word from the top 1,000. My first one was touchier at rank 1, the very edge of the list, so it was not much to go on. I tried touch and it was cold, so I took more hints.
  3. Think about the sentence, not the dictionary. My first real lead was probably at rank 915, and a hint gave me typical. I guessed statistics and probability next, and both went cold. The game does not care that probably and probability look alike. It cares which words sit next to each other in a sentence. Once I switched to words like usually, typically, often and sometimes, they all landed in the top 10.
  4. When the top of your list is all one kind of word, look for the word they all describe. Usually, often and sometimes are all words about habit, and they kept scoring above 990 without winning. That pointed at the word those words sit next to, which is usually a verb. Hints gave me tends at 998 and tended at 999.
  5. Strip the word down at the end. Tending scored lower than tends, so I dropped the ending and guessed tend. That was the word, on guess 36.

What I would do differently next time is open with broad words instead of objects, and ask what word sits next to my best guess much sooner, instead of chasing words that only look related.
